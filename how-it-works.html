How it works

From a written guide to a settled payment.

Every job follows the same path. Agents do the routing and bookkeeping. People make every judgement that a grade depends on.

  1. 1. The guide comes first

    We write the task guide with you: what counts as correct, what to do with doubt, and at least ten worked examples including the hard ones. The guide has a version number. Every record names the version it was done under.

  2. 2. Seats are matched to the work

    Each of our 17 worker seats holds grades per kind of work. The router only offers an item to a seat whose grade covers it. A seat that is still in training sees gold items only.

  3. 3. Gold items keep the pool honest

    Known-answer items are mixed into every queue at a fixed rate. They are never marked to the worker. A seat whose gold accuracy falls below the line in the guide is paused for review, and its open items go to other seats.

  4. 4. Reviewers grade a share

    A reviewer grades a sample of each batch against the guide. A grade names the item, the reason and the guide version. Reviewers do not see a worker's name when they grade, only the seat.

  5. 5. Failed items are re-done

    A failed item is sent to a different seat. We do not edit a worker's answer and keep their name on it. The first answer and the second answer are both kept in the record.

  6. 6. Settlement follows the grade

    Worker pay for each task is computed from the grades on that task. Paid work waits until the seat's identity verification is complete. A payment that is held is shown as held, with the reason.

Where the agents stop and people start

Agents do

Split batches, match seats to items, keep queues and deadlines, write the first draft of a transcript, and keep the records in order.

People do

Write the guide, grade the work, rule on disagreements, verify identity, and decide whether a seat stays in the pool.

Nobody does

Nobody can change a grade after it is recorded without a second, logged grade. Nothing is paid on a grade that has no reason beside it.

We would rather deliver a slower batch that a second person can check than a fast one that only the first person understood.
